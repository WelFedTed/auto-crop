# 1. Vision, scope and decisions

Status as of 2026-09-30: planning only, no source code exists. Decision IDs **B1-B21** (owner decisions), resolution IDs **D1-D9** (1.6.1) and assumption IDs **C1-C7**, **A-1 to A-12** and **P1-P3** (1.7) are referenced throughout PLAN.md and ROADMAP.md. Where research and a decision disagree, the decision wins and the text says how the risk is mitigated.

## 1.1 Elevator pitch and tagline

**Tagline: Auto Crop - the offline batch fixer for scans, receipts and photos.**

Auto Crop is a free, open-source (MIT OR Apache-2.0) desktop app for Windows, macOS and Linux. Drop in images or a folder and it finds the page, receipt or photo, crops it, rotates it, removes skew and perspective distortion, and offers to whiten the paper and sharpen the text of black-and-white documents. It also converts formats, first of all HEIC/HEIF to JPG. Results land in a review grid where anything the app is unsure about comes first, and every change can be adjusted with large touch-friendly handles and undone. Everything runs locally: no account, no upload, no telemetry.

The scope is narrow on purpose: the images you already have, fixed in bulk, with the app saying honestly when it is not sure. It is not a scanner app, an image editor or an OCR tool (1.3).

## 1.2 Goals and success criteria

Every number is **PROVISIONAL** unless a decision fixes it, and none has been measured; they become gates in ROADMAP.md once the benchmark and accuracy harness exists (built before any GUI work). A **silent failure** is an auto-accepted result with any of: quad IoU below 0.90 after the canonical warp; ink clipped by more than 2 px; not upright, or skew above 1.0 degrees; a no-document image that was cropped; a wrong item count or an item IoU below 0.90 (definition and measurement: 07 §7.4). The 0.95 and 0.98 figures are reported success levels (0.95 is also the agreement bar between the two detectors, 04 §4.3.3), never the project definition. The widely quoted 0.945 SmartDoc "success" threshold has no traceable source and is not adopted.

## 1.3 Non-goals

Explicit for 1.0; they never appear as checkboxes in ROADMAP.md.

- **OCR and searchable-PDF text layers (B19).** PDF export is image-only.
- **Scanner and camera capture (B19):** TWAIN, WIA, SANE, webcam.
- **Watch-folder auto-processing (B19).**
- **A mobile app (B19, C3).** The core stays UI-agnostic so it remains possible.
- **General photo editing (B19):** filters, retouching. Enhancement exists only to make documents legible.
- **Cloud, accounts, sync, telemetry or silent auto-update (B18).** The tool overwrites user files, so it never updates itself silently.
- **Every format literally.** "All image formats" (brief item 11) is delivered as a defined support matrix (P1 in 1.7).
- **HEVC or HEIC encoding (B12).** Only decoding is bundled (libde265 inside a decode-only libheif); x265 and x264 are never linked.
- **Preserving HDR gain maps, depth maps or Live Photo video (B12, C2).** HEIC to JPG tone-maps to SDR and drops them with a visible notice.
- **ML enhancement (B13).** Enhancement is classical in 1.0; an empty, dormant on-demand model slot is the only hook.
- **Paid signing or any purchase (B15).** The budget is $0: no paid Windows certificate, no Apple Developer ID, no bought HEVC extension.
- **Radial lens correction (A-11).** The brief's "distortion" is delivered as perspective correction and curved-page dewarp (B10); barrel and pincushion correction needs per-lens profiles and is a 1.x candidate (04 §4.12).

Non-goals must not paint the architecture into a corner: the data model carries N quads per image and edits are a parametric versioned EditState with pluggable stages, so OCR, capture or watch folders can be added later.

## 1.4 Target users and the four input scenarios

**Who it is for.** (1) People digitising paperwork from a phone: receipts, invoices, forms. (2) People with a flatbed scanner and a pile of documents or family photos. (3) Anyone holding HEIC files that a website or printer will not accept. (4) Scripters who want the engine as a headless CLI (B10).

The four B1 scenarios each get a benchmark slice:

| Scenario | Typical input | Job to be done | Hard cases |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 Phone photo of a document or receipt | 12-48 MP JPEG/HEIC, perspective, shadows, clutter | "Make this look like a scan, and tell me if you are unsure" | Receipts above 4:1, thermal fade, white paper on a white desk, partial frames |
| S2 Flatbed scan of a document | 150-600 dpi TIFF/JPEG/PNG, small skew, lid borders | "Trim, straighten and whiten a stack of pages" | Near-blank pages, dark lid edges, pages scanned rotated |
| S3 Multi-photo flatbed scan | Several prints or receipts per scan | "Give me each item as its own straight, cropped file" (multi-item split; touching or doubtful items are always held, clear splits follow the B4/B6 triage once the multi-item gate passes) | Touching or overlapping items, wrong item count |
| S4 General photo | Camera or phone photos, HEIC, EXIF-rotated | "Fix orientation, drop borders, convert to JPG" (EXIF/irot applied once, conversion under C2) | HDR/gain-map HEIC, Display P3, Live Photo, panoramas |

## 1.5 Product principles

1. **Fast.** Cheap proxies decide; full resolution refines. Rust owns every pixel; the webview only draws a proxy, overlays and gestures. Fixed pyramid: 256 px thumbnail, 1024 px detection proxy, analysis proxy of about 1.5 MP by area (long edge at most 3072 px), 2-4 MP display proxy plus 512 px tiles. During a drag the webview applies a geometry-only matrix (frame median at most 17 ms, p95 at most 20); on release Rust renders the real result (final render within 150 ms p50 and 250 ms p95, cross-fade about 150 ms).
2. **Honest confidence.** Three bands shown as icon plus word, never a raw score. Good: calibrated score at or above the mode's cutoff t, auto-saved (A-3). Check: 0.60 up to t, held, listed first, original untouched. Failed: below 0.60 or a hard gate, original untouched with a "Draw crop" banner. t is 0.95, 0.90 or 0.80 for Strict, Balanced or Aggressive until `calibration.json` exists, then fitted on real data; 0.9 is a research start value, not the Balanced cutoff (07 §7.4). A wrong crop costs more than no crop, so low confidence means "leave unchanged and flag" (B4).
3. **Offline and private.** All processing and models are local, with no telemetry. Network use is a manual update check and an opt-in weekly notify-only check (B18).
4. **Touch-first, not touch-only.** Corner handles 48 px nominal and never under 44, other controls at least 24 px, separate Review and Adjust modes, a loupe for corner dragging, always-visible buttons with gestures as accelerators. Mouse, trackpad and keyboard work everywhere; touch is guaranteed on Windows and macOS, best-effort on Linux with documented limitations, never "unsupported" (B8). Density follows the last-used pointer, since Windows 11 has no tablet-mode signal.
5. **Safe overwrite with backup.** Overwriting is the default (B3), so the safety net must exceed what "save copy" would need: verified output, backup first, atomic replace, restore after restart (commit protocol: 02 §2.7).
6. **Accessible and international.** Screen readers get names and status, never colour-only state. Externalised strings, a pseudo-locale in CI and RTL-capable layout from day one (B20).
7. **Non-destructive.** Output is a pure function of (source, EditState); undo is cheap because edits are parameters, not pixels.

## 1.8 Rust as the backend: an honest opinion

Rust is the right choice for the core, but not because it is faster. The fastest pieces are C or SIMD libraries: libjpeg-turbo, libheif and ONNX Runtime. The one important Rust speed win is `fast_image_resize`, about 13x faster than `image`'s resizer on Lanczos3 (a single-thread desktop-CPU benchmark; expect less on laptops). Rust's real gains are memory-safe parsing of hostile files, `rayon` parallelism, easy cargo packaging and the `ort` ecosystem.

Caveats, so nobody expects a "pure Rust" project:

- HEIC, PDF and any RAW support still need C or C++. `catch_unwind` cannot stop a segfault inside libheif, so those decoders run in a worker-process pool; Rust's safety does not cross that boundary.
- We must write or port what the ecosystem lacks: a line-segment detector (port only from OpenCV's BSD-style version, or use EDLines), Sauvola with u64 strip integrals (imageproc's u32 integrals overflow above about 16.8 MP), a u8 Lanczos warp (spike `kornia-imgproc`'s f32 Lanczos first) and a TIFF G4 writer.
- `ort` is a release candidate, and `turbojpeg-sys` needs CMake and NASM and vendors a stale libjpeg-turbo, so "no C toolchain" is false; hence the devcontainer and `xtask`.
- The real risk is the GUI and webview, not the language.

C++ with Qt and OpenCV would be faster to prototype the vision code but gives up memory safety on arbitrary input. `opencv-rust` is used only as a dev-only test oracle. If the pure-Rust kernels miss the harness gates, the response is a reopened decision, not a silent switch.

## 1.9 Competitive landscape and differentiators

No maintained tool combines automatic crop, deskew, perspective correction, black-and-white clean-up, a review UI, undo and HEIC conversion in one cross-platform app.

| Tool | Status | Gap Auto Crop targets |
|---|---|---|
| ScanTailor family (GPL-3.0) | Original archived 2020; several forks, one released in 2026 | Mouse-era Qt UI, no HEIC, no document quad or perspective. GPL: study only |
| NAPS2 (GPL-2+) | v8.3.2, 2026-07-22, healthy | Strong at scanning, PDF and OCR. Deskews only freshly scanned pages. No auto-crop ([#226](https://github.com/cyanfish/naps2/issues/226)) or perspective correction; a bounds-only auto-crop PR ([#830](https://github.com/cyanfish/naps2/pull/830)) is open |
| ImageMagick, unpaper, OCRmyPDF | Current; unpaper 7.0.0 dates from 2022 | `-deskew` is anecdotally small-angle only; unpaper is CLI-only and FFmpeg-coupled; OCRmyPDF is PDF-centric |
| Photoshop, Lightroom, VueScan, ScanSnap | Proprietary and paid; VueScan and ScanSnap are scanner-tied | Multi-photo split needs clean gaps; over- and under-crop reports; Photoshop and ScanSnap have no Linux |
| Mobile scanners | Ads, watermarks, subscriptions; [Microsoft Lens](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/lens/) stopped new scans 2026-03-09 | Phone-first; desktop batch users are underserved |
| iMazing, CopyTrans HEIC | Free tiers | iMazing has no Linux; CopyTrans is Windows-only, JPEG-only, 100 per batch |

Reuse verdict: only permissive code (OpenCV, Leptonica, imageproc, MIT projects such as jscanify) can seed our codebase; GPL work is an idea source only.

**Differentiators (seven).**

1. **A confidence-ranked review queue instead of silent bad crops** (B4-B6), against rivals' top complaint of silent over- and under-crop.
2. **One non-destructive edit stack** from detect through enhance and convert, with undo, instead of a chain of tools.
3. **Touch-first correction:** corner handles, loupe, pinch and rotate. Rivals' touch support was not fully checked, but none we found advertises it.
4. **Published, reproducible benchmarks** on synthetic and public data (SmartDoc and CORD are CC BY 4.0); the private golden set adds aggregates only (B21).
5. **Multi-item split** of several receipts or photos per scan, the top Photoshop complaint and a paid VueScan feature.
6. **Any image or folder, HEIC done properly:** a bundled decoder means no codec purchase (B12); orientation is applied once, ICC read first, and the metadata policy is visible (C2). Gain-map handling beyond the drop-with-notice rule (1.3) is an unverified opportunity.
7. **No ads, watermark, account, subscription or telemetry,** and real Linux support.

Not a moat: NAPS2 could gain a bounds-only auto-crop; the lasting edge is quad detection, multi-item split, review UX and measured speed.

## 1.10 Research corrections applied

Popular claims in the research that were wrong or overstated; this plan uses the corrected versions.

- **Slint fallback.** The gesture handler arrived in Slint 1.16 (not 1.14) and file-path drops in 1.18 (not 1.16); OS folder drops exist only in the Qt backend; RTL mirroring is unsupported ([#2294](https://github.com/slint-ui/slint/issues/2294)). The fallback gate therefore includes a folder-drop test.
- **Tauri.** The updater supports deb, rpm, AppImage, NSIS and MSI, not AppImage only. The bundler does not produce Flatpak. wry already disables WebView2 pinch page-zoom, so only Linux pinch is a real problem.
- **Licensing.** Slint's Royalty-free licence does allow an MIT or Apache app ("MIT forces GPL" was wrong), though it is non-OSI. LGPL requires relinkability, not dynamic linking as such. UVDoc's "MIT dataset" covers repo tooling only; its textures are third-party. DocTr and DocGeoNet are non-commercial.
- **HEIC.** The "$41/month sponsorship" misread "41 past sponsors": say "almost no recurring funding". `libheif-sys` embedded mode bundles 1.23.1 (current 1.23.5) and the Windows vcpkg path still inherits GPL x265. "No pure-Rust decoder exists" was too strong (`heic-rs` is MIT or Apache but weeks old). iPhone ICC versus nclx is unresolved: read ICC first, then nclx.
- **Geometry.** SmartDoc's 0.688 on background 5 is the average of all 2015 entrants, not the best system; later methods reach 0.98 overall. zune-jpeg has no DCT scaling, and vendored libjpeg-turbo 3.1.0 is stale (3.1.4 fixes a `tj3Transform` double-free). PP-LCNet's 99.06% comes from a 1,000-image ID and document set.
- **Enhancement.** The DocEnTr figures (Otsu 77.7, Sauvola 77.1, DocEnTr 92.5) belong to DIBCO 2017; on H-DIBCO 2018 they are 51.5, 67.8 and 90.6. Bako et al. never evaluated receipts. "JBIG2 is 3-5x smaller than G4" is unsupported. Tesseract's Sauvola window is about 100 px at 300 dpi, so sweep well beyond 31-51 px.
- **Architecture.** `rayon` aborts only for `spawn` and `spawn_fifo`, and no case covers C segfaults; `persist()` does not fsync the parent directory.
- **Distribution.** A self-submitted Homebrew cask needs 90 forks, 90 watchers or 225 stars (not 75). `macos-26-intel` exists, so the August 2027 Intel cut-off is uncertain.
- **Performance and UX.** `ort` 2.0.0-rc.13 wraps ONNX Runtime 1.28, not 1.30. The 84 ms turbojpeg-per-6-MP figure is unsourced and the stage budgets did not sum, so all budgets are PROVISIONAL and were revised. Genius Scan's "about 10% manual correction" is unsupported (its numbers imply about 15%).
- **Prior art.** NAPS2 does deskew scanned pages, and the 4lex4 ScanTailor fork's master dates from 2020, not 2023.

## 1.12 How to read PLAN.md and ROADMAP.md

- **PLAN.md is the reasoning:** what we build, why, and what could go wrong. It cites decisions (B1-B21), resolutions (D1-D9, 1.6.1), assumptions (C1-C7, A-1 to A-12, P1-P3) and spikes as gates.
- **ROADMAP.md is the living checklist and effective to-do list.** Items have stable IDs `M<n>.<nn>` grouped by milestone; each milestone has a Goal, Release, Depends on, Size (part-time weeks: S 1-2, M 3-4, L 5-8, XL 9-16), Exit gate, Risks / spikes and checklists by area. GATE items cite the gate IDs of 07 §7.11 (G0-G11), and owner-decision assumptions appear as "A-n" and are never re-opened there. Boxes are ticked with the work, only when implemented and verified; IDs are never renumbered or reused, and a retired item's ID simply stops appearing in the list; non-goals are plain bullets.
- **PROVISIONAL** marks any number not yet measured; treat it as a hypothesis until a benchmark replaces it.
- **Decision beats research.** Where the plan follows a decision over a research recommendation, it says so and names the mitigation; where an independent verification contradicted a researcher, the verification is used. Genuinely open questions become spikes with gates in ROADMAP.md.
